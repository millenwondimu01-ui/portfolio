Navigating Human Bias in AI Leadership

Working in cybersecurity has taught me that making quick decisions can sometimes lead to the wrong conclusion. When you're looking at alerts or reviewing data, it's easy to assume you already know what's happening. I've learned that taking a step back and asking a few more questions often leads to a better outcome. As I move into data analytics and AI, I want to carry that same mindset into my leadership. I want to be known as someone who listens, stays open-minded, and doesn't let assumptions make decisions for me.

One thing I would do is encourage people to challenge my ideas. I don't think good leaders need to have every answer. Sometimes the best way to reduce bias is to hear a perspective different from your own. I also think AI projects should be reviewed regularly instead of assuming they will always be fair. Data changes over time, and if no one checks the results, biased patterns can go unnoticed.

Another lesson I've learned is that numbers don't always tell the whole story. Before making decisions based on analytics, I want to understand the people behind the data because every chart or report represents someone's experience. My goal is to use AI to support better decisions while remembering that people, not technology, are responsible for making ethical choices.
